Held in Common.

How your work lives on a node — and how a commons is made.

The whole idea in one line

Your work stays with you.

It lives on a node you run. A commons only ever holds a copy you chose to send — or nothing at all.

Govern the models, not only the data.

Model A · everyone publishes to one directory

The shared commons.

  • Each person runs a node
  • They share a copy to one commons
  • The commons is the directory everyone browses
  • Simple, searchable — one place to look

Model B · you choose where it travels

Federated nodes.

  • Your node is yours — publish locally first
  • Share to the shared commons
  • …or to a friend's commons
  • …or keep it on your own metal (a DGX, a laptop)

The key move

A node is a commons.

The same software runs your node and receives others' shares. Point people at your node and you are their commons — on your own terms, on your own hardware.

How the data relates.

  • You hold a handle and a key — your identity
  • Your profile points to your works and datasets
  • Every change is a version with a permanent fingerprint
  • Each version carries a license + CC signals

How nodes form a commons.

  • Nodes that share into one inbox are a commons
  • Anyone can run one — a studio, a festival, a class
  • A commons is just a node others point at
  • Commons can federate to each other

Who can see it.

Solid = it reaches there · dashed = it never does

Why a share can be trusted.

  • Every share is signed by your key
  • It carries a content fingerprint (cid)
  • The commons re-checks both — tampering is rejected
  • Members-only is encrypted to the member set

In short

You hold it in common.

  • Your node, your keys, your terms
  • Share to a commons — or keep it home
  • Signed, fingerprinted, and governed by you
