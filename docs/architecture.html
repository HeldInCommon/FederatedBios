Nothing is uploaded to a company. Your work lives on your own machine. You decide what leaves, who sees it, and how it may be used.

1
Where your work lives

Storage.

Your work rests on a node you control.

First word: node

A node is
an app you run.

You install Held in Common on your own computer — Mac or Linux. It opens like any app and holds your work at full quality. No company account; your keys stay on your machine.

What's inside your node — the data model

How your work is held.

Read it left to right: you hold a handle and a key. Your profile points to your works and datasets. Every change is saved as a version with a permanent fingerprint — and each version carries its license and signals.

2
How it moves

Circulation.

You choose how far each piece travels.

Second word: commons

A commons is
a shared room.

Anyone can host one — your collective, a festival, a gallery. You bring copies in; the originals stay in your studio. Walk out and they're still yours.

And it's the same app: point your node outward to receive, and you're hosting a commons too.

Say you've just finished a series

How far should it travel?

Just me
It stays in your studio. Nothing is shared at all.
My collective
Members-only — encrypted so only the people you name can open it.
Collaborators
In the commons, shared by link — present, but kept out of search.
A festival or gallery
Public — listed in the commons and open for anyone to find.
Your collective's circle

Locked to the
people you name.

Members-only work is scrambled so only your collective's keys can open it. It never leaves your studio readable — not even the commons can see inside, and it's never used to train a model.

How studios share — the architecture

Nodes federate
into a commons.

You send a signed copy to a commons; your original never moves. It gathers everyone's copies into one directory people can browse — no central vault, no landlord.

How you actually use it

From install to shared, step by step.

01
Open the app
It starts your node on your computer and opens its page in your browser.
02
Make a handle
The app creates your keys and keeps them on your machine. No email, no company.
03
Add your work
Each piece gets a fingerprint and your signature, stored on your node.
04
Choose its reach
Public, commons, or members-only — encrypted to the people you name.
05
Send it to a commons
Your node posts a signed copy; the commons checks the signature and fingerprint, then lists it. Members-only never leaves.
Why it can be trusted

No one can
fake your work.

Every copy is signed with your key and carries a fingerprint. Change one character and the commons rejects it. The seal is how a stranger knows a work is really yours.

3
What the machines may learn

Learning.

Models may learn — only on your terms.

Govern the models, not only the data

Consent rides
with the data.

A model can learn from the commons — but only within the terms each work carries. If you said credit me, or ask first, that choice travels with every copy. Members-only work is never used to train at all.

4
Who makes the rules

Govern.

The community decides, in the open.

You set the terms

No landlord.
You hold the rules.

A Creative Commons license says how people may reuse your work. CC signals say how AI may learn from it — Credit, Direct contribution, Ecosystem, Open. The people who share decide these together, out loud.
