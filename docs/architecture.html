Held in Common.

Your work lives on a machine you control — and travels only as far as you say.

Why we built this

You made the work.
You should keep the say.

Creative work is being swept up to train AI — usually without credit, consent, or a way to say no. Held in Common flips that around: your work stays with you, and you attach the terms it travels under.

Your work trained it. Now who owns what it learned?

Two words you'll hear all day

Node & commons.

A NODEis the app running on your own computer. It holds your profile and your work. It's yours — nobody else can see what's on it unless you send them a copy.
A COMMONSis a shared directory other people can browse. You send a copy of your profile to it — or you don't. A commons can be run by anyone: a studio, a class, a festival, even another artist.

Your node is home. A commons is a place you choose to be seen.

The simplest shape

Everyone runs a node · everyone sends a copy to one place

One shared commons.

The easiest way to start. Each person runs their own node, then shares a copy of their profile to a single commons — the directory everyone browses.

Good for: finding each other quickly, one place to look.

The fuller shape

The same node, three choices — you decide each time

You choose where it goes.

Publishing happens on your node first. Then, for each profile, you decide: push a copy to the shared commons, to a friend's commons, or keep it on your own hardware — a laptop, a studio server, a DGX.

The idea that ties it together

A node is a commons.

There's no special "server" software. The same app that runs your node can also receive other people's shares. So the moment you let others point at you, you are a commons — hosting your scene on your own terms, on your own hardware.

Anyone can host a commons. Even you.

What's inside a node

How your data relates.

Read it left to right: you hold a handle and a key (the app manages the key — you never touch it). Your profile points to your works and datasets. Every change is saved as a version with a permanent fingerprint, and each version carries a license and CC signals.

Scaling out

How nodes form a commons.

A cluster of nodes sharing into one place is a commons. There can be many — a studio, a festival, a class — each on its own terms. And commons can federate to each other, so work can travel between scenes without anyone owning the whole thing.

Your call, every time

Who gets to see it.

Solid = it reaches there · outline = it never does

  • Public — in the directory and open to search engines.
  • Commons — in the directory, but kept out of search. Shared by link.
  • Members-only — encrypted, only signed-in members can read it. It never leaves your node unencrypted.
Why it can be trusted

No one can fake your work.

Every share is signed with your key and carries a fingerprint of its contents. The receiving commons checks both — so if a single character is changed in transit, it's rejected. And members-only shares are encrypted to the member set, so even the commons can't read them.

Govern the models, not only the data

You set the terms for AI.

Each version carries two things anyone reusing it can read by machine: a Creative Commons license (how people may reuse the work) and CC signals — your preferences for how AI models may learn from it.

  • Credit — attribution expected
  • Direct contribution — give back to the creators
  • Ecosystem — sustain the open commons
  • Open — keep what's built on it open

The community sets the terms of use — together, and in the open.
